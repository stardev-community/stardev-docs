Software licensing for indie developers

Most indie products do not need licensing — they need a payment link. Licensing starts to matter when the product can be copied, resold, or shared, and when support costs grow with every copy.

Do you need it?

  • Sold as a download or script? Yes, probably.
  • SaaS where the value is server-side? No — accounts already gate it.
  • Free with donations? No.

Three options

Build it yourselfInfra onlyWeeks, plus maintenance

Hidden costs

Hosted: plan ceilings, price changes, and a queue for edge cases. Self-hosted: backups, key rotation, and deploy discipline. Build: the same maintenance, plus the bugs you have not hit yet.

A decision checklist

  1. How many customers in 12 months? Under 50, keep it manual.
  2. Will customers ask for device resets? If yes, self-service matters.
  3. Does anyone ask where key data lives? Then hosting matters.
  4. What is one hour of your time worth? Multiply by setup and support.

If you go self-hosted

Keep the client contract to two fields — valid and expiry — and everything else on the server. That single decision is what makes migration or a rewrite cheap later.
