KeyAuth alternative: what you give up, what you gain

KeyAuth works. The problem is rarely the API — it is what happens when your product outgrows a plan, the price changes, or the service has a bad week. This page is about the trade, not about pretending hosted services are bad.

When staying hosted is the right call

  • You ship one small product and want zero infrastructure work.
  • You are fine with a dashboard and do not need custom logic.
  • You would rather pay monthly than think about Redis, backups, and deploy keys.

When self-hosting starts to pay off

  • Your own customers submit HWID resets and you want to handle them without a vendor queue.
  • You need behaviour the dashboard does not expose: custom expiry rules, extra metadata, unusual rate limits.
  • You sell to people who ask where their key data lives.
  • You are already paying, and the yearly total is bigger than a one-time source purchase.

What migrating actually looks like

The client side is small: one request, one boolean, one expiry. The work is mapping your key records and HWID history into the new store, then keeping the same response shape so old clients do not break.

POST /api/verify
{ "key": "XXXX-XXXX", "hwid": "ab12...", "app": "my-script" }

200 { "valid": true,  "expiresAt": 1767225600, "plan": "user" }
200 { "valid": false, "message": "Key expired" }

Keeping that contract stable is what makes a migration a weekend job instead of a rewrite. It is also why a self-hosted stack is easier to leave later: the endpoint is yours.
